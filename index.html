So, what am I actually subscribing to?

Two physical letters a month, sent to your address in India. The contents will vary: a story, a playful activity, a thoughtful piece of writing or a little glimpse of somewhere else. The letter itself is the keepsake.

Do I get to choose what’s inside?

The surprise is part of the ritual. The hints and sample here show the tone; the exact contents stay under wraps until you open your envelope.

Is this for me?

We’re planning this subscription for adults aged 18 and over, of any gender. If you enjoy real letters, small adventures and a little time to yourself, we’d love to write to you.

Will every letter be handwritten?

We’re still working out the paper and production details. The sample shows our writing style. We’ll confirm exactly what’s handwritten and what’s printed before subscriptions open.

Can I send a letter to a friend?

We love that idea too. A service to post your words to someone you love is on our future wish list. It won’t be part of the first subscription.

When can I join, and what will it cost?

The launch date and pricing are being worked out. The first 50 paid subscribers will receive the founding offer. We’ll confirm the offer duration, postage, delivery coverage and cancellation terms before checkout opens.

A LETTER ABOUT AN UNEXPECTED COMEBACK

Dear you,

In April, I got fired for the first time in my life. I had a lot on my mind and, suddenly, a very accommodating calendar.

There are four of us in this story: three friends who went to college together and a boyfriend we’ll call Mr. A. One of the girls lives abroad and was home for a visit, so we planned a night away, a couple of hours outside Mumbai. A weekday worked beautifully for me, for obvious reasons. She was on holiday, and Mr. A’s availability tends to align remarkably well with hers.

Our other friend couldn’t make it, which is how a group getaway became a romantic break with me included in the booking.

There was a pool, which wouldn’t normally have been relevant to my interests. I’d spent three and a half years living near a beach and barely got into the water. The strange thing is that I remember being a good swimmer as a child. Somewhere along the way, I’d become convinced that I’d forgotten how. In my head, putting me in water had roughly the same outcome as throwing in a stone.

That evening, while my friend and Mr. A were busy being a couple, I got into the pool. And somehow, I started swimming.

I don’t mean I immediately became an Olympic prospect. I mean some part of me remembered what to do. A movement worked, then another, and I kept going because I couldn’t quite believe it was happening. After feeling so lost outside the pool, finding something familiar in it felt very good.

We’d been drinking that evening, so afterwards I wasn’t entirely sure how much credit to give my apparent comeback. Mixing that with a pool was poor judgement; the part worth repeating came later.

A few months later, I booked a villa with a huge pool for my birthday. When I got in, the feeling was still there. I was more comfortable in the water, and those movements were coming back. Naturally, I started telling my friends about this remarkable development. My body was remembering things. I was becoming a new person. You can imagine how I was going on.

Then one friend offered a slightly less spiritual explanation.

“Maybe you wanted to get away from their conversation so badly that you remembered how to swim.”

For context, my friend and Mr. A are a chaotic couple. They can turn almost anything into an argument, and I had been their captive audience on a weekday getaway. It is entirely possible that my body looked at the situation and decided we needed another mode of transport.

I would love to tell you this is a story about determination. There is a reasonable chance it is a story about wanting to be at the other end of a pool.

Either way, I’ve now taken about seven swimming classes in the past month, and I’m swimming lengths without a float. Something I thought I’d lost has become something I look forward to doing again.

So I’m leaving a little room for surprises, including the ones that arrive when life is a mess and your friends are being deeply annoying. I didn’t come home with a new job or a plan for my life, but I did come home wanting to get back in the water.

I’m tempted to recommend finding yourself some chaotic friends. Apparently, mine come with an unexpected extracurricular programme.

With love and questionable company,
Audacity

Based on a true story, with names withheld.
