Two letters a month, posted to your address anywhere in India. One might be a story, another might be a little dare, or something to keep. What we’ve shown on this page are possible themes, not a fixed list of what’s in every envelope.

Nope. Not knowing is half the fun.

Who’s it for?

Anyone 18 or older who likes real post and a bit of time to themselves.

Are the letters handwritten?

They’re written by real people, the founders of Audacity. No AI writes your letters. We print them so they arrive neat and easy to read, but every word comes from us.

When does it start, and what does it cost?

Letters start going out on 1 December 2026. It’s ₹450 for a month (two letters) or ₹1,199 for three months (six letters). We only write to 30 people a month, so book early.

No. It’s a surprise, and tracking numbers ruin surprises.

What if a letter doesn’t arrive?

We post through India Post, so now and then something gets delayed or lost. Tell us within 30 days of the end of that month. Once we’ve checked, we’ll send you a replacement the following month, so you always get every letter you paid for.

Can I cancel or get a refund?

No. Once you’ve paid, your spot is locked in and we can’t cancel or refund it.

Three things. A letter to a friend (₹599), a letter to your future self (₹299), and A Sign, by Post (₹499), a letter for you right now, from whoever you need to hear from.

Type it, upload a Word doc, or record a voice note and we’ll write it up. For friend letters, you can also use our writing helper to get a first draft.

Can I send it anonymously?

Yes. For friend letters, you choose whether to sign it with your name or keep it anonymous. We won’t tell them who sent it.

We do, the founders, based on what you tell us. It’s written as if it came from the person you chose. It isn’t really from them, and it isn’t professional, medical or legal advice.

Dear you,

Okay, sit down. Chai optional but recommended.

In April, I got fired. First time ever. Very humbling. On the bright side, my calendar had never looked so relaxed.

So here’s the cast. Three college friends, and one boyfriend we’ll call Mr. A, because he’d be thrilled to be in a letter and I refuse to give him that. One friend lives abroad and was home for a bit, so we planned a night away, a couple of hours outside Mumbai. A weekday? No problem for me, for reasons already covered. She was on holiday. And Mr. A’s free time has a funny way of matching hers exactly.

Then our fourth friend dropped out. Which is how a girls’ trip became their romantic getaway, with me as the bonus guest.

There was a pool. Normally, a pool and I don’t interact. I lived near a beach for three and a half years and barely went in. The thing is, I was a decent swimmer as a kid. Somewhere along the way, I’d decided I’d forgotten how. In my head, me in water and a stone in water ended the same way.

That evening, while the two of them were busy being a couple, I got in. And I swam.

Calm down, I’m not going to the Olympics. But some part of me just remembered. One stroke worked, then another, and I kept going mostly because I couldn’t believe it. After months of feeling lost on land, finding something familiar in the water felt really, really good.

Full disclosure, we’d had a few drinks. So I wasn’t sure how much credit to give myself. (Also, drinks and pools are a terrible combination, please don’t. The good part comes later.)

A few months later, I booked a villa with a huge pool for my birthday. Got in. Still there. Easier, even. So naturally, I told everyone. My body was remembering things. I was becoming a new person. You know how I get.

Then one friend offered a slightly less spiritual explanation.

“Maybe you wanted to get away from their conversation so badly that you remembered how to swim.”

Some context. My friend and Mr. A can turn anything into a fight. Anything. And I had been their captive audience on a weekday getaway. It’s very possible my body looked at the situation and decided we needed another way out.

I’d love to tell you this is a story about determination. It’s at least half a story about wanting to be at the other end of the pool.

Either way, I’ve done about seven swimming classes this past month, and I’m swimming lengths without a float. Something I thought I’d lost is now something I look forward to.

So I’m leaving a little room for surprises, including the ones that show up when life is a mess and your friends are being deeply annoying. I didn’t come back with a new job or a plan for my life. I came back wanting to get in the water again.

I’d almost tell you to go find some chaotic friends. Apparently, mine come with a free extracurricular programme.

With love and questionable company,
Audacity
